# Adjudication of CR-26 — the profiler amendment (2026-08-19, independent adjudicator)

Applies to `docs/2026-08-19-cr26-profiler.md` (branch `cr26-profiler`, `f49c42b`) and its paired
empyrean draft (branch `profiler-amendment`, `a4aa302`, editing `contract/protocol.md` — the new §6
`profiler` group, the §11.16 entry — and `contract/schema/bus-protocol.schema.json` — three fragments
and two `limits` keys). Every anchor below was re-verified against the file it names in this session —
against the trees and commits themselves, never against the CR's own citations. No emulator MCP tooling
was used; no cargo was run; no tree's checked-out branch was switched (all reads via
`git show <ref>:<path>`; this ruling was committed from a temporary worktree). The only things executed
were JSON and git.

## Ruling: **ADOPT WITH CHANGES**

The core of this CR is sound, and most of it is verified to the byte. The two "verbatim" blocks are
**byte-identical** to the empyrean branch (diffed mechanically: 120/120 and 100/100 lines). The schema
count is exactly as claimed (**33 → 36** fragments, 34 → 37 `methods` keys, parsed from both revisions),
the three added keys are exactly the three profiler methods, and the claim that the amendment touches
the schema in "exactly two other places" is structurally true: after removing the three fragments, the
two `limits` keys, and the `description` recount, the remaining schema is byte-equal to the
pre-amendment revision. `Draft202012Validator.check_schema` passes; I rebuilt the CR's twenty message
classes independently and added eleven adversarial cases of my own — **31 of 31 landed as the fragments
intend** (exclusive `budgetPct`/`budgetPctOmitted` pair, `dependentRequired` in both directions, the
`symbolName` `+$hex` refusal, `truncated`-even-when-false, `additionalProperties: false` on rows and
buckets, closed-composition accept/reject). All three legacy-instrument defect citations reproduce at
their stated lines, the stall enumeration matches what `bus.rs` actually attributes — including the
negative claims — and the four ambiguity resolutions and the one deliberate weakening are the right
calls, each grounded where the CR says it is grounded, with one mischaracterized premise (M5).

But the amendment's normative §6 text carries **one literal self-contradiction and one off-by-one
ambiguity in the divisor definition**, its interrupt rule leaves one edge where two conforming servers
could legitimately diverge, the migration list omits the one delta that would make the consumer's first
migrated call refuse, and the CR repeats the lineage's known failure mode twice (a wrong anchor, a
recon claim its own arithmetic contradicts). The last two documents in this lineage audited clean;
CR-25's adjudication made an eleven-line anchor drift a MUST. Held to that bar: adopt-with-changes.

## MUST (blocks adoption; each is a mechanical text edit)

Because the CR presents the §6 group and §11.16 entry as byte-identical extractions, **any MUST that
edits the empyrean text also requires re-extracting the CR's fenced blocks afterward** so the
byte-identity claims stay true. M1–M3 edit the amendment; M4–M6 edit the CR document only.

**M1 — Resolve the `-32005` self-contradiction in the ninth behaviour.** The blockquote says
*"None of the three may be refused with `-32005` on a free-running machine"*
(`protocol.md:1400` @ `a4aa302`), while the tenth behaviour in the same blockquote **requires**
`get_profiler_frames` to be *"refused with `-32005` (`data.reason = "perFrameNotArmed"`)"* when
`frames` is passed with no ring armed — a refusal that applies regardless of run state, so on a
free-running machine the two sentences issue contradictory MUSTs for the same request. The fragment's
own `$comment` already has the correct wording (*"never refused with -32005 **for run state**"*).
Fix at both prose sites:

- `protocol.md:1400`: *"None of the three may be refused with `-32005` on a free-running machine:"* →
  *"None of the three may be refused with `-32005` for the machine's run state:"*
- `protocol.md:2860` (§11.16 entry, change column): *"synchronous arm/disarm/read that no server may
  refuse with `-32005` on a free-running machine"* → *"… may refuse with `-32005` for the machine's
  run state"*.

**M2 — Fix the off-by-one in `frameCount`'s definition.** `protocol.md:1358`: *"It is the number of
whole frames in the sample, incremented at each boundary observed while armed"*. The sample *"opens at
the first frame boundary after arming"* (`:1340-1341`), so *n* boundaries observed while armed delimit
**n − 1** whole frames: the two halves of the sentence disagree by exactly one, and the number is the
divisor the whole blockquote tells clients to read rather than assume. Two implementers can today
conform to different divisors. Fix: *"incremented at each boundary observed while armed **after the
one that opened the sample**"* (or equivalent words excluding the opening boundary from the count).

**M3 — Pin the interrupt bucket's two unstated edges.** The rule (opens at acknowledge, closes at the
matching `RTE`, nesting by depth) is complete for nesting but leaves two cases a real ROM produces
undefined, and on the first of them two conforming servers could diverge:

1. **An `RTE` with no open bucket.** An interrupt taken *before* the sample opened (arm inside a
   handler; a handler in flight at the opening boundary) has no bucket; its `RTE` arrives with nothing
   to match. Nothing says whether a server may retroactively open a bucket at sample-open (one honest
   reading) or must close nothing (the other).
2. **The `RTE` of a non-interrupt exception inside a handler.** A `TRAP` taken inside a VBlank handler
   returns via `RTE`; a depth counter that counts every `RTE` closes the `vint` bucket early — the
   reference's own W3-class bug, one row up. *"Matching"* carries the intent but does not exclude it.

Fix: one added sentence in the `interrupts` bullet (after *"…inclusive of everything the handler
called."*, `protocol.md:1369`), e.g.: *"The matching `RTE` is the one that unwinds that interrupt's
own exception frame: the `RTE` of an exception taken inside the handler (a `TRAP`, an address error)
closes that exception and not the bucket, and an `RTE` with no open bucket — an interrupt taken before
the sample opened — closes nothing; a handler the sample never saw enter accrues to no bucket."*
Exact wording is the drafter's; both edges MUST be pinned.

**M4 — The migration list must say the `frames` argument itself goes, not just the `- 1`.** CR §10 is
billed as *"deltas the demand side must be told, not left to discover"*, but delta 2 says only *"The
`- 1` goes away (`:535`)"*. The probe's call is `get_profiler_frames{"frames": sample - 1, "top": 200}`
(`raster_cost_probe.py:535` @ aeon `18af84f3`); deleting only the `- 1` leaves `frames: sample`, and
under the amendment any `frames` param with no per-frame ring armed is **refused with `-32005`
(`perFrameNotArmed`)** — and the demand side answered *record-and-later*, so no ring will be armed.
As written, the first migrated run refuses, which is precisely the "left to discover" this section
exists to prevent. Fix delta 2 to state that the parameter is dropped entirely (the migrated call is
`get_profiler_frames{"top": 200}`), with the refusal named as the reason.

**M5 — Correct the mischaracterized recon premise in §6 item 9.** The CR writes: *"its own §G.2 sketch
puts per-frame figures on the rows — the two disagree."* §G.2's example is arithmetically coherent
**only** as totals-on-rows: its `vint` bucket has `calls: 31` over a 31-frame sample (one VBlank per
frame — a total, not a per-frame 1), and its example row's `cycles: 318460` exceeds the result-level
`cyclesPerFrame: 119394`, which is impossible for a per-frame row figure. The *real* conflict — which
fully justifies the resolution taken — is between recon §F.2/W13/W14 (*"Rows carry exact integer
totals over the sample"*, *"totals are totals"*) and the floor the demand side consumes: the legacy
row's `cycles` is a per-frame figure (`ControlSocket.cpp:2041`), read off the row and gated with `==`
(demand doc §1.2–§1.5), so totals-on-rows would silently multiply the consumer's expectations by the
frame count. The resolution (rows keep the floor's per-frame meaning; `sampleCycles` carries the
undivided total; `F-PROF-TOTALS` registered) is **affirmed**; the premise sentence must describe the
actual conflict rather than attribute to §G.2 a shape its own numbers rule out.

**M6 — Fix the `protocol.md:2593` anchor for the CR-23 limits precedent.** Cited twice (CR §5 prose;
CR §11 table). In the pre-amendment file the CR-23 row is at `protocol.md:2477`; post-amendment it is
`:2595`. `:2593` matches neither frame — post-amendment it is **CR-21's** row (which happens to
contain the string `limits.maxWriteLen`, the likely grep trap). Every other protocol.md anchor in the
CR is pre-amendment; change both occurrences to `:2477` (or cite both frames explicitly).

# Delta ruling — the second post-adjudication delta (2026-08-19, same adjudicator)

Applies to the CR's delta section (`cr26-delta2` @ `712bdb4`) and the paired empyrean commit
(`profiler-amendment` @ `64fc3f8`, atop `a4002fb`), against the implementation the delta transcribes
(`profiler-s1` @ `218ae58`). Same method and constraints as the first round: everything re-verified
against the trees themselves via `git show`; no cargo (a verification run holds the lock); no emulator
tooling; the only things executed were JSON and git. Before auditing the delta I verified `a4002fb`
applies the first round's M1–M3 faithfully at every named site — it does, including both fragment
`$comment` alignments and the §11.16 column.

## Ruling on the delta: **ADOPT WITH CHANGES**

The delta's substance is right and unusually well-evidenced: the fold-at-boundary-checkpoint deviation
is real, correctly reasoned (a report-time fold would sweep in the post-last-boundary tail that
`sampleCycles` excludes — verified in `profiler.rs`: cycles reach `committed_*` only at boundaries, and
`report()` reads committed state exclusively), flagged rather than taken quietly, and ruled in.
`calls`-counts-completions, the `calls: 0` main-loop row, the typed counters, and `unattributedCycles`
are all faithful transcriptions of code that exists and is mutation-tested. Both re-extracted blocks are
byte-identical again (184/184 at `:1303-1486`; 141/141 at `:2908-3048`); the fragment count is unmoved
at 36 (parsed at all three revisions: 33 → 36 → 36); `64fc3f8` touches no fragment but
`get_profiler_frames` (structural diff); `check_schema` passes and a 14-case delta harness of mine
reproduced every claimed schema behaviour, including all three old-shape refusals. The 182-line shift
and the `2907`-lines-at-that-point coordinate both recount correctly.

But the delta's centerpiece — the normative reconciliation identity — is written over a wire key that
does not exist, and the same fragment it amends **refuses** that key; one of its logical connectives
overclaims against its own floor bound; and its safety argument's headline predicate is falsified, read
literally, by §7 of the same document. Hence adopt-with-changes a second time.

## MUST (blocks adoption; text-level and mechanically applicable)

Because the CR's fenced blocks are labelled byte-identical, **D-M1 and D-M2 edit the empyrean text and
therefore require re-extracting both blocks afterward**, as the delta itself did once already.

**D-M1 — The identity's bucket term is not on the wire, and the fragment refuses the key the identity
tells clients to sum.** The normative identity (`protocol.md:1327` @ `64fc3f8`) and its client-side form
(`:1333-1334`) sum `interrupts[].cyclesSelf` in wire spelling; the fragment's new `$comment` says the
sum "on the wire reconstructs" from it; `unattributedCycles`' description repeats it; §11.16's delta
paragraph and adoption clause 7 (`:3036`) say "Σ `cyclesSelf` over rows and buckets". But the
`interruptBucket` schema is `{cycles, stallCycles, calls}` with `additionalProperties: false` — I
verified mechanically that **a bucket carrying `cyclesSelf` is refused by the very fragment this delta
amends**. As amended, the contract simultaneously directs a client to compute a sum and refuses any
reply that would let it. The in-tree clause-7 test only closes because it reads the internal `Report`,
whose buckets *do* carry `self_cycles` (`profiler.rs`, `Counts`, divided in `report()`) — which is also
the proof that the natural fix is cheap. Fix, one of, applied consistently at every site (§6 identity
paragraph, client-form sentence, fragment `$comment`, `unattributedCycles` description, §11.16 delta
paragraph, clause 7, CR delta §(a)):

  1. *(recommended)* Add `cyclesSelf` to the interrupt bucket: the §6 row becomes
     `interrupts{hint,vint}{cycles,cyclesSelf,stallCycles,calls}`, `interruptBucket` gains the property
     (integer, min 0, REQUIRED, same-keying description as the row field). The same pre-release safety
     argument covers it — it is the fourth required addition in the same window, and it is a field the
     implementation already computes and divides.
  2. Restate every identity site over keys the wire carries, and demote the client-side reconciliation
     to a server-side/conformance-suite property. This forfeits client-side reconciliation — which the
     delta's own rationale for the typed counters ("a client reconciling rows") leans on — so (1) is
     the coherent choice; (2) is listed because the choice is the controller's, not mine.

**D-M2 — "closes exactly *only* when `perFrameExact` is `true`" overclaims, against its own floor
bound.** `perFrameExact` is true iff **every** divided figure in the reply divided evenly — including
`calls`, `stallCycles`, `totalCycles` and the bucket figures. The `cyclesSelf` reconstruction can
therefore close exactly while `perFrameExact` is `false` (some *other* figure truncated), and the same
sentence's own bound — "low by **at most** `frameCount − 1` per figure" — permits a zero shortfall. As
written, a conformance author can derive `!perFrameExact ⟹ reconstruction ≠ sampleCycles`, which the
text's own arithmetic refutes. Fix at all three sites (`protocol.md:1334`, the fragment `$comment`, CR
delta §(a)): "is **guaranteed** to close exactly when `perFrameExact` is `true`; when it is `false`
each divided figure is floored, so the reconstruction may fall short — by at most `frameCount − 1` per
figure, never over."

**D-M3 — The safety argument's headline predicate is false read literally, and §7 of the same document
proves it.** "**no server implements these three methods yet and no client has ever received one of
their replies**" (`protocol.md:3009`; CR `:441`) — the legacy C++ socket serves all three method
*names* today, and the consumer's probe receives `get_profiler_frames` replies from it in every run;
the CR's own §7 item 4 says so ("they go on talking to the legacy server exactly as before"). The
argument is sound only under its intended scoping — no server *speaking this bus* implements them, and
no reply *any fragment governed* has ever been received — so say the scoping at both sites, e.g.: "no
server speaking this bus implements these three methods and no client has ever received a reply a
fragment governed — the legacy socket's rows are the pre-§11.16 floor, never stamped and never
conformant (§8)." With that fix the precedent is narrow enough that it cannot be cited post-release:
the predicate is factual and the expiry is operational ("the day after the first server ships one, the
same edit is a breaking change").

## SHOULD (recorded, non-blocking)

**D-S1 — The "exactly one source" claim has a second, dead write site in the code, whose comment
believes it is live.** `charge()`'s refused-root fallback (`profiler.rs`, the `else` after the
empty-stack root push) adds to `pending_unattributed` and comments itself as the stack-capped case —
but it is unreachable today: an empty stack always accepts the root push (`MAX_DEPTH` = 16384 ≥ 1).
The contract's single-source claim is therefore observationally true, and I verified the other
candidates feed rows, not the escape hatch (abandoned frames checkpoint into their rows before the
uncompleted pop; depth-cap cycles charge to the innermost tracked frame; the opening boundary discards
pre-open pendings entirely). But a refactor that makes that arm reachable would silently mint a second
source with no test to catch it and the contract's enumeration already stale. Implementation ledger:
make the arm's unreachability explicit (or assert it), so the contract's "exactly one source" stays
load-bearing.

**D-S2 — Scope the counters' span, or extend the empty-sample sentence.** The empty-sample sentence
predates the three fields. At `frameCount: 0`, `unattributedCycles` is provably `0` (nothing is
committed before the second boundary) — worth one clause, since the identity's degenerate case is the
reader's first sanity check. `abandonedFrames`/`depthExceeded`, by contrast, count events **since
arming**, including the pre-open span the sample itself excludes (`self.abandoned`/`self.depth_exceeded`
are not reset at the opening boundary), and the contract nowhere says which span they cover. One
sentence in the eleventh behaviour ("counted since arming, not since the sample opened — they are facts
about the instrument's whole watch") pins it before two servers diverge.

**D-S3 — The coordinator's brief mislabels `total_stall_cycles` as undivided; the ruling record should
not inherit it.** In `Report`, `sample_stall_cycles` is the undivided stall total and
`total_stall_cycles` is that figure **divided** by `frameCount` (`report()`, `div(self.committed_stall)`).
Recorded here so the open item is transcribed right when the handler slice picks it up.
