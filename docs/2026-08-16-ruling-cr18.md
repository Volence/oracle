Un-framed adjudication of `docs/2026-08-15-cr18-sprite-table.md`, the last open §8 item-19 violation.
Same mechanism as `docs/2026-08-15-fable-ruling-cr9-cr11-cr12.md`: the adjudicator was given the CR, the

The design survived intact. **Every error was in the CR's evidence, not its conclusions**, which is the

## What it added that the CR had not considered

- **`cacheDivergence` sets the opposite convention** from `pixel_attribution.sprite.cacheDivergence`,
  which is "Present only when true" (schema:851). Always-present is still right (§11.5's `exact`
  re-ruling), but the divergence must be recorded and the alignment registered as a follow-up.
- **`satAddr` is not actually inconsistent** between the two rows: `pixel_attribution` carries no
  `satBase`, so the address is not derivable there. Same rule, two answers — worth a sentence so the rows
  don't read as drift.
- **Three unpinned normatives**, each a place a second implementation could differ defensibly: the order
  of `sprites[]` (slot order, index-ascending, **never link order** — and the *other* instrument is
  link-ordered, so this is the one place they could be confused), `total` (**80 always**, not `parsedMax`),
  and `limit`'s bounds (1–80, per §11.8's "two policies wearing one name").
- **`caveat` must be actively declared absent** (§2.4 rule 4), with the envelope's `running: true` as the
  answer.
- **`watchpoint_hits`, not `watchpoint_list`, is the shape precedent** — it is the adopted row carrying
  scalars beside a flat list, which is what this row does.
- **Adoption is conditional on the fragment being *executed*, open and closed**, not merely written —
  §11.6 and §11.8 both proved that rule on themselves.
- **D15's parity rule does not reach the walk either**: a renderer consuming its own evaluation record is
  not "a capability its GUI consumes." A second, independent reason the scope boundary is right.

## What it ruled on the open question

**`limit` alone; no `index` param**, on the asymmetry of reversal — an optional `index` added later is
additive, one adopted now can never be removed — and because the panel's one-sprite-by-index flow is
*already* served by `pixel_attribution.sprite`, which returns the full decode in the same reply as the
index. Reversal condition, in the `sinceSeq` idiom: adopt `index` when a client is **measured** paying for
whole-table reads it does not want.

## Where it agreed

The violation is real (zero-hit grep reproduced), the two-instrument scope boundary is correct and
correctly placed, the 64-vs-80 resolution is right with both alternatives rejected for the right reasons,
all four D9 type assignments are correct, the two struck fields are correctly struck, and the three
behaviour pins follow CR-10's precedent — including the third being correctly *different* from it.

## The lesson, which is the same one as last time

**The mechanism earns its cost on claims, not conclusions.** Sixteen changes, zero of which altered the
design; one of them was a sentence I invented and quoted. A CR is read once and then lives in a permanent
log that later work cites as settled fact — which is exactly why the register's own history is of striking
false provenance rather than bad decisions.
