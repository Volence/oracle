## 1. Why this is being raised

### The rows exist, and nothing has ever checked a reply against them

`empyrean/contract/protocol.md:1311-1313`, verbatim as they stood before this CR:

```
| `emulator/set_profiler`        | `enabled`         | `enabled` |
| `emulator/get_profiler`        | —                 | `enabled`,`framesRecorded`? |
| `emulator/get_profiler_frames` | `frames`?,`top`?  | `frameCount`,`totalCycles`,`budgetPct`,`routines[]`,`interrupts{}` |
```

Three facts follow, and together they are the reason a CR comes before a handler:

1. **The catalogued shape is the legacy C++ server's, warts included.** `budgetPct` is that server's
   hardcoded NTSC `128000` written into the contract (`oracle/linux-port/gui/ControlSocket.cpp:2033`);
   `routines[]` and `interrupts{}` have no field-level specification at all; and *status / misc* is not
   where a measurement instrument belongs.
2. **There is no schema fragment.** The schema's own top-level `description` names `profiler` among *"the
   deferred families … completed as each is implemented, per §8 item 20: a server's suite closes every
   result against its fragment, so a method without one cannot ship a result nobody has checked"*
   (`contract/schema/bus-protocol.schema.json:5`).
3. **`UNCOVERED_METHODS` is pinned empty** in the reference suite
   (`crates/oracle-aether/tests/schema_conformance.rs:142`, `const UNCOVERED_METHODS: &[&str] = &[];`), so
   advertising a profiler method without a fragment turns that test red. The contract-first ordering here is
   enforced by a gate, not requested by a convention.

### The demand, and the cost of not answering it

Aeon's cost-gate lane runs on the old C++ oracle **and only there**, because *"oracle-next has no profiler
instrument at all"* — their words, quoting an answer this repo gave them on 2026-08-18
(`docs/2026-08-19-aeon-profiler-demand.md` §0). The migration gap is exactly these three rows; every other
call their probe makes already exists on this bus (demand doc §0's table). The instrument they are stuck on
has cost them measured time. Their own runner records *"the oracle stop-race intermittently wedges ONE
emulator-backed gate forever"* (`aeon/tools/effects_gates.py:56-66`, at aeon `18af84f3`, read today for this
CR; quoted without the emphasis the demand doc's transcription adds), and a whole self-re-invoking segmented
architecture exists because of it.

### The three structural defects this shape corrects — verified firsthand for this CR

## 3. The proposed §6 text (verbatim, as it landed on the branch)

A new `### profiler` group, inserted immediately before `### status / misc`; the three rows are removed from
that table. **This block is byte-identical to `contract/protocol.md:1303-1535` on `profiler-amendment` at

```markdown
### profiler
*(The three rows below were catalogued under **status / misc** with a legacy shape and no schema fragment.
**§11.16** (CR-26, 2026-08-19) re-specified them, gave them their first fragments, and moved them here: the
method names are unchanged, and a measurement instrument is not status.)*

| Method | params | result |
|---|---|---|
| `emulator/set_profiler` | `enabled` (bool), `perFrame`? (bool, def false) | `enabled`, `perFrame` |
| `emulator/get_profiler` | — | `enabled`, `perFrame`, `framesRecorded`, `routineCount` |
| `emulator/get_profiler_frames` | `frames`? (≥1, ≤`limits.maxProfilerFrames`), `top`? (≥1, ≤`limits.maxProfilerRoutines`) | `frameCount`, `sampleCycles`, `totalCycles`, `unattributedCycles`, `abandonedFrames`, `depthExceeded`, `perFrameExact`, `budgetPct` \| `budgetPctOmitted`, `routines` (§2.4 container of `{addr,name?,disp?,cycles,cyclesSelf,stallCycles,calls,cyclesTotal,cyclesSelfTotal,stallCyclesTotal,callsTotal}`), `interrupts{hint,vint}{cycles,cyclesSelf,stallCycles,calls,cyclesTotal,cyclesSelfTotal,stallCyclesTotal,callsTotal}`, `perFrame`? (§2.4 container of `{frame,cycles,stallCycles,hintCycles,vintCycles}`) |

> **What the profiler measures, and what a row means** *(added 2026-08-19, §11.16)*. `set_profiler` arms an
> exact per-invocation CPU-cycle accountant, `get_profiler` reports its state, and `get_profiler_frames`
> returns the accumulated sample. Twelve behaviours are normative.
>
> - **Attribution is exact, and a row is keyed by a routine entry address.** Every cycle the sample retired
>   is attributed **exactly once** — to a routine row, to an interrupt bucket, or to `unattributedCycles`.
>   `cycles` is **inclusive** of callees — the meaning the legacy rows had in practice, kept so a migrating
>   consumer's arithmetic does not change — and `cyclesSelf` is the same span with callee time subtracted.
>   A server MUST NOT satisfy these rows by PC sampling: they are compared with `==` by the consumers this
>   surface exists for, and an estimator cannot be equality-gated.
>
>   **The reconciliation identity, and it is normative.** Over the **undivided** sample:
>
>   > Σ `routines[].cyclesSelf` + Σ `interrupts[].cyclesSelf` + `unattributedCycles` == `sampleCycles`
>
>   exactly, with no tolerance. It is stated on *self* rather than inclusive cycles because inclusive time
>   double-counts by construction, and because a parent's share of a callee still in flight across a
>   boundary lands in the parent's row only when that callee returns — self has no such lag. The wire
>   carries the identity's own terms **undivided**, so a client can check it **exactly, always**:
>
>   > Σ `routines[].cyclesSelfTotal` + Σ `interrupts[].cyclesSelfTotal` + `unattributedCycles` ==
>   > `sampleCycles`
>
>   Same equation, over keys of this reply, with **no condition on `perFrameExact` and no floor bound**.
>   Every term is REQUIRED and undivided — which is why `cyclesSelfTotal` is carried on the interrupt
>   bucket as well as on the routine row — so a conforming client can reconcile a reply against itself
>   exactly, and a suite can gate on it without a fixture engineered to divide evenly.
>
>   The **divided** view has the weaker form, and the weakness belongs to the view rather than to the
>   accounting: `(Σ routines[].cyclesSelf + Σ interrupts[].cyclesSelf) × frameCount + unattributedCycles ==
>   sampleCycles` is **guaranteed to close exactly when `perFrameExact` is `true`**; when it is `false`
>   each divided figure is floored, so it may fall **short — by at most `frameCount − 1` per summed figure,
>   never over** — and it may still close on the nose then anyway, because that flag reports truncation in
>   *every* divided figure in the reply (`calls` and `stallCycles` included) and not only in the ones this
>   sum touches. `unattributedCycles` and `sampleCycles` are themselves undivided, so neither is affected.
>   **A client checking the identity should use the undivided form**; the divided one is stated because it
>   is what a per-frame view can promise, not because it is the check to write.
>
>   **`unattributedCycles` is what keeps the identity closed rather than leaking a shortfall.** It has
>   exactly one source: the in-sample time of an interrupt that was **already in flight when the sample
>   opened**, whose bucket the interrupt behaviour below forbids opening retroactively (*"a server MUST NOT
>   retroactively open one"*). Those cycles are
>   real, they are inside `sampleCycles`, and they belong to no bucket — so they are reported rather than
>   dropped. It is normally `0`, and a non-zero value is ordinary rather than alarming: a VBlank handler
>   straddling the opening boundary is the common phase, not a fault.
>
>   **`calls` counts COMPLETED invocations.** A routine still running when the sample closes **has a row** —
>   carrying every cycle it accrued, checkpointed at each frame boundary — with `calls: 0`. A main loop that
>   never returns is the expected shape of that row, and reporting nothing for it while its cycles inflated
>   `sampleCycles` would be the worst of both. A server MUST NOT invent a completion for a call it did not
>   observe end; the cycles are the measurement, and the count is a count.
>
> - **`stallCycles` is the part of `cycles` the CPU spent held off the bus rather than executing.** It is a
>   **subset of `cycles` on the same row, keyed identically to it** — same routine key, same inclusive
>   span, same in-server division — so `cycles - stallCycles` is a well-formed subtraction and not two
>   quantities with different aggregation glued together. Exactly three conditions count, enumerated rather
>   than described so that a fourth is a visible amendment and never a silent widening under green gates:
>   **(i)** a 68000 write to the VDP data port held off while the write FIFO is full; **(ii)** a 68000 read
>   of the VDP data port waiting for that FIFO to drain; **(iii)** the whole bus-hold window of a 68k→VDP
>   DMA, billed to the instruction that armed it. A VRAM fill and a VRAM copy contribute **nothing**,
>   because the 68000 keeps running through both. A server whose bus stalls the CPU for a reason not on
>   this list raises an amendment before counting it here: a client subtracting `stallCycles` to recover an
>   ideal-cycle figure is entitled to know exactly what it subtracted.
>
> - **Division happens inside the server, and the sample is delimited by frame boundaries at both ends.**
>   Every row's `cycles`, `cyclesSelf`, `stallCycles` and `calls`, both interrupt buckets, and
>   `totalCycles` are **per-frame figures over the sample** — the exact sample total divided by
>   `frameCount`. `sampleCycles` carries the undivided total for the whole sample. The sample **opens at
>   the first frame boundary after arming and closes at the most recent one**, so every frame it covers is
>   a whole frame; cycles retired before the opening boundary or after the closing one are attributed to no
>   reported frame. **A partial first frame is therefore inexpressible**, which is what retires the
>   hand-applied "ask for one frame fewer than you ran" compensation the legacy surface required of its
>   callers.
>
> - **Every row figure is also published undivided, and those are the exact ones.** Beside the four
>   per-frame figures each routine row and each interrupt bucket carries **`cyclesTotal`**,
>   **`cyclesSelfTotal`**, **`stallCyclesTotal`** and **`callsTotal`** — the same four quantities over the
>   whole sample, before any division, REQUIRED and keyed identically to their divided partners. They are
>   not second measurements: when `frameCount > 0`, `divided == total / frameCount` under integer division
>   for each pair, so a total also **bounds** its partner's truncation —
>   `divided × frameCount ≤ total < (divided + 1) × frameCount`. With `frameCount: 0` there are no rows, so
>   the relation is never left undefined on a reply that has any.
>
>   **Why a count in particular could not wait.** Division truncates a cycle figure; it frequently
>   *destroys* a count. A routine invoked 4.53 times per frame reports `calls: 2`, and one invoked once
>   across a 31-frame sample reports `calls: 0`. Both are correct — a count is never floored up — and
>   neither can be equality-gated against a fire count derived from engine source, which is the consuming
>   pattern this surface exists to serve; a consumer facing exactly that had to source every rate in a
>   root-cause packet from an engine-side counter that happened to exist. **`callsTotal` is that number,
>   exactly, regardless of `perFrameExact`.**
>
>   **And why the other three ride with it rather than waiting for a defect report.** A `==` gate on a
>   count fails *loudly* — the number is visibly wrong, which is how the count gap was found. A `==` gate
>   on cycles fails *quietly*: a shortfall of at most `frameCount − 1` against a row of tens of thousands
>   of cycles reads exactly like agreement. Publishing all four undivided closes the loud gap and the quiet
>   one in the same window, and it is what lets the reconciliation identity above be stated with no
>   `perFrameExact` condition at all.
>
>   **They are not carried on `perFrame[]` rows.** Those hold whole-frame totals with no per-routine
>   breakdown and are already undivided, so there is nothing to un-divide; a total there would be a second
>   spelling of the same number. The four fields live in exactly two shapes — the routine row and the
>   interrupt bucket — and that is a bound on where they may appear, not an omission.
>
> - **Truncation is reported, never absorbed, and nothing is fabricated.** The divisions are integer
>   divisions. **`perFrameExact` is `true` if and only if every divided figure in the reply divided without
>   remainder**; when it is `false` each divided figure is floored — one-sided low, bounded by one unit per
>   figure — and a client gating with `==` MUST check it, because `perFrameExact: true` is also what makes
>   `cycles - stallCycles` exact arithmetic rather than the difference of two truncations. A count is never
>   floored **up**: a routine invoked fewer times than the sample has frames reports `calls: 0`, and a
>   server MUST NOT substitute `1` for it, MUST NOT substitute `1` for a zero denominator, and MUST NOT
>   answer an empty sample with an error. An empty sample is `frameCount: 0`, empty lists, every divided
>   figure `0` and `perFrameExact: true` — there is nothing to divide and nothing was lost.
>   `unattributedCycles` is `0` there too, necessarily: nothing is committed to the sample before its second
>   boundary, so the identity's degenerate case is `0 + 0 + 0 == 0`. `abandonedFrames` and `depthExceeded`
>   are the two that may be non-zero in an empty sample, because they count since arming (below).
>
>   **Expect `false` in real samples.** On a running game almost nothing divides evenly across a
>   multi-frame window, so `perFrameExact: false` is the ordinary reading and `true` is the special case a
>   synthetic fixture produces. That is the text working as written rather than a defect, and it is said
>   here so a consumer does not read the common case as an alarm — but it does mean a gate that compares a
>   divided figure with `==` should either drive a deterministic fixture or compare undivided
>   (`sampleCycles`, and per-row figures reconstructed as above).
>
> - **`frameCount` is counted, and it is the number to divide by — read it rather than assume it.** It is
>   the number of **whole** frames in the sample: incremented at each boundary observed while armed **after
>   the one that opened the sample**, which is never counted, because the span before it is not a frame the
>   sample saw. *n* boundaries observed therefore delimit **n − 1** counted frames, and an armed instrument
>   that has seen one boundary and no more reports `0`. A server MUST NOT derive the number by subtracting
>   frame indices, which are positions on a clock `reset` restarts (§2.2).
>   `get_profiler.framesRecorded` is the same number seen from the snapshot method, and the two MUST agree
>   when no frames were run between the calls. Whether a client that ran `S` frames sees `S` or `S − 1`
>   depends on where in the frame it armed, and that is precisely why the count is on the wire.
>
>   **One dilution a client must know about before dividing by this number.** A single 68k→VDP DMA can hold
>   the bus across **more than one** boundary. When it does, every frame it spans receives a share of what
>   was, to the program, one indivisible act — so a per-frame figure over such a sample is *diluted* rather
>   than wrong. A consumer comparing against a per-frame ideal should read `sampleCycles` with `frameCount`
>   instead of the divided row. This is a property of dividing by frames at all, not of the accounting.
>
> - **`interrupts` is keyed by interrupt CAUSE, never by handler address.** `hint` is the level-4 (HBlank)
>   bucket, `vint` the level-6 (VBlank) one, and a server MUST classify by the interrupt it acknowledged
>   rather than by where the handler happens to live: an address heuristic mis-buckets for any ROM whose
>   vector points somewhere it did not anticipate, and a silently wrong number is worse than a missing one.
>   A bucket **opens when the interrupt is taken** — the acknowledge cycle, not the raise — and **closes at
>   the matching `RTE`**, and its `cycles` is inclusive of everything the handler called. **The matching
>   `RTE` is the one that unwinds that interrupt's own exception frame**: the `RTE` of an exception taken
>   *inside* the handler — a `TRAP`, an address error — closes that exception and **not** the bucket, and an
>   `RTE` that finds no open bucket closes nothing. An interrupt the sample never saw taken, because it was
>   taken before the sample opened, accrues to no bucket at all: a server MUST NOT retroactively open one,
>   since a bucket whose entry was never observed would report a cost the sample cannot account for.
>   `calls` counts the times the interrupt was **taken**: a raised-but-masked HBlank appears nowhere. **Nesting is by depth and
>   never double-counted** — cycles retired while a nested HBlank runs inside a VBlank handler belong to
>   `hint` alone, and the suspended `vint` bucket accrues nothing until the nested one closes. The split is
>   additive to per-routine rows, not a replacement: a handler's entry address still gets its own
>   `routines[]` row.
>
> - **`addr` is canonical; `name` is the bare label and `disp` is a number.** `addr` is a hex string (D9
>   category 1) of the routine's entry address **masked to the 24 address lines the 68000 drives**, so a row
>   key and a listing address compare directly instead of through a client-side mask. `name` is §4's
>   identifying spelling with no `+$hex` suffix, ever, and `disp` is the displacement past it — `0` in the
>   normal case, where the row key is exactly a label. The two are **absent together** when nothing
>   resolves: a server MUST NOT fall back to the address string, because a client cannot tell a symbol
>   spelled like an address from a lookup that failed.
>
> - **`budgetPct` is derived from the machine's own `timingBasis`, or it is not emitted.** It is
>   `totalCycles` as a percentage of one frame's CPU-cycle budget, and that budget MUST be computed from
>   the `timingBasis` this same server advertises (§2.1, D16) — `mclkPerFrame` over the master clocks per
>   CPU cycle — never from a hardcoded constant, which is wrong by ~16% the moment the machine is PAL. Read
>   it as a **sanity check on the accounting** rather than a measure of load: on a machine whose CPU is
>   never halted it sits at ~100%, and a materially lower figure means cycles went somewhere the sample does
>   not attribute. If the basis changed inside the sample the derivation is ambiguous, and the server MUST
>   **omit `budgetPct` and emit `budgetPctOmitted`** naming the condition instead of averaging over the
>   change. Exactly one of the two keys is present.
>
> - **Arm, disarm and read are synchronous with the command, and none of the three requires a paused
>   machine.** A server MUST apply `set_profiler` before it replies: a client that arms, runs frames and
>   reads in three consecutive calls MUST see the sample those calls describe, with no sleep, no poll and
>   no extra frame needed for the arm to take effect. `set_profiler{enabled:true}` **resets the
>   accumulators** — there is no resume in this revision, and a second arm therefore discards an in-flight
>   sample — while `set_profiler{enabled:false}` **retains** the sample so it can be read afterwards, and
>   reading never clears. None of the three may be refused with `-32005` **for the machine's run state**:
>   the sample's edges are frame boundaries, not the instant the command landed, so a free-running arm is
>   exactly as well defined as a paused one, and both reads are pure. That exemption is about run state and
>   nothing else — the `perFrameNotArmed` refusal in the next behaviour is a refusal about the *instrument's*
>   state, and it applies whether the machine is running or paused.
>
> - **`routines` and `perFrame` are §2.4 policy-bounded containers, and their bounds are refused rather
>   than clipped.** Both take the nested container spelling `{items, total, returned, truncated, limit?}`,
>   because each is a *field* of a larger result rather than the whole of it; neither method accepts a
>   cursor, so neither may emit one (§2.4 clause (b)). `top` bounds `routines.items`, `routines` is ordered
>   by `cycles` descending so that a truncated list is the expensive end rather than an arbitrary slice, and
>   a `top` above the server's cap is **refused with `-32602`, never clamped** — the cap is a policy number,
>   so it is advertised as **`limits.maxProfilerRoutines`** (REQUIRED when the method is advertised, on the
>   `maxWriteLen` / `maxHashLen` precedent). **`perFrame` is opt-in**: present if and only if the sample was
>   armed with `set_profiler{perFrame:true}`, carrying whole-frame totals only (no per-routine breakdown)
>   and no division, since a per-frame row is already per frame. Its ring depth is
>   **`limits.maxProfilerFrames`**, required on the same condition. `frames` bounds the list to that many
>   most-recent frames, is refused above the ring depth with `-32602`, and is **refused with `-32005`
>   (`data.reason = "perFrameNotArmed"`)** when the ring was not armed, because a parameter that cannot
>   affect the answer is worse than one that is rejected. No `pct` key is emitted anywhere: the legacy one
>   divided inclusive cycles by a frame total and summed far past 100%, and a client that wants a share divides `cyclesSelf` by `totalCycles`, which is the ratio
>   that sums to at most 100.
>
> - **`abandonedFrames` and `depthExceeded` are typed counters, always present, and `0` is their honest
>   quiet value.** They are facts about the **measurement**, not about the program, and a client
>   reconciling rows is entitled to both machine-readably rather than in prose. `abandonedFrames` counts
>   frames torn off the shadow stack without completing — a routine an `RTE` unwound past, or one displaced
>   by a stack the server could not follow: **their cycles are still reported** (they ran) and their `calls`
>   are not (they never finished), so a non-zero value says some row's `calls` understates its invocations.
>   `depthExceeded` counts calls the server declined to track because its shadow stack was at its bound;
>   those cycles are charged to the innermost tracked frame, so nothing leaves the totals and the identity
>   still closes, but the rows they would have had do not exist and the frame they charge to is overstated.
>   Both are **counted since arming**, not since the sample opened: they are facts about the instrument's
>   whole watch, so an event in the pre-open span the sample itself excludes still shows here — which is the
>   honest direction, since the alternative hides a lost stack behind a boundary. Both are **undivided** —
>   they count events, not per-frame rates — and both are REQUIRED even when `0`,
>   for §2.3's reason: absence and zero must not both mean "nothing happened". A server SHOULD emit a
>   `caveat` naming whichever is non-zero, and MUST NOT emit one for them when both are `0` — §2.4's
>   advisory, applied: a caveat present on every reply is one clients learn to ignore.
>
> `get_profiler_frames` MAY emit a `caveat` (§2.4) — conditionally, as on an empty sample — and its
> fragment declares the key; the row above does not enumerate it, per §2.4 rule 4.
```

2. **`cycles` inclusive, `cyclesSelf` additive.** Inclusive is the floor's meaning in practice, and the
   demand side confirmed on 2026-08-19 that every reader they have consumes rows inclusively. Both ship
   (ruling Q3).
3. **`stallCycles` ships in v1**, not in a later slice — the ruling promoted it. It is a subset of `cycles`
   on the same row, keyed identically, so `cycles - stallCycles` recovers the ideal-cycle figure the older
   instrument reports.
4. **The stall definition is enumerated, not described.** Written from what this server's bus actually
   attributes, read today: a data-port **write** held off while the write FIFO is full
   (`crates/oracle-core/src/bus.rs:977-988`), a data-port **read** waiting for that FIFO to drain
   (`:956-971`), and the whole bus-hold window of a **68k→VDP DMA** billed to the instruction that armed it
   (`:1019-1047`, returned as CPU wait cycles at `:1047`). A VRAM **fill** and a VRAM **copy** contribute
   **nothing** — the 68000 keeps running through both, stated in the code at `:1003` and `:1008` — and every
   other arm of `read16` / `write16` / `read8` / `write8` returns a wait of `0` (`:1094`, `:1121`, `:1155`,
   `:1172`), including the Z80 window. The demand side asked for this pin explicitly so that a future stall
   source is a visible schema and amendment event rather than a silent widening under green gates.
5. **Interrupt buckets keyed by CAUSE, with the nesting rule pinned** (ruling Q6): the bucket opens at the
   acknowledge cycle, closes at the matching `RTE`, nests by depth, and a nested HInt inside a VInt
   attributes to `hint` while the suspended `vint` accrues nothing. `calls` counts interrupts **taken**, not
   raised. Cause-keying costs this server zero core change, because the fc = 7 acknowledge is already on the
   sink stream (`crates/oracle-core/src/bus.rs:1063`; corroborated in
   `crates/oracle-core/src/watchpoints.rs:439`).
6. **`name` bare + `disp` numeric**, per ruling Q5, which sidesteps the question by house precedent rather
   than deciding it: §4's rule that *"a displacement is never inside a name string"* and the
   `$defs/symbolName` retype §11.6 applied to `read_memory.symbol` (`protocol.md:2162-2165`). Since no
   `name+$hex` composite is ever emitted, `$defs/symbolName` conformance is structural here, and
   `F-SYMBOLNAME-DISP` — the recon's flagged divergence between `symbol_at`'s rendering and that pattern —
   stays TAGged without blocking this arc.
7. **`budgetPct` derived from `timingBasis`** (ruling Q2), never a hardcoded constant.
   `crates/oracle-core/src/system.rs:61-80` is the `TimingBasis` struct and its NTSC constant — derived from
   the scheduler's own constants, so the reported basis and the frame arithmetic cannot disagree — and
   `:548` is the accessor whose *value* goes live when region becomes machine state without its signature
   changing. Q2's second half is the `budgetPctOmitted` key: *"refuse the ambiguity explicitly in the reply
   rather than averaging over it"*, which §2.4 rule 3 then requires be a typed key and not a caveat.
8. **`perFrame[]` ships opt-in and bounded** (ruling Q1), with D13-style cap refusal.
9. **Rows carry the per-frame figures; the result carries the undivided total.** *(Resolution.)* The
   conflict is between the recon and the floor, not inside the recon: recon §F.2 and warts W13/W14 put
   **exact totals on the rows** — *"Rows carry exact integer totals over the sample"*, *"totals are
   totals"* — while the legacy row's `cycles` is a **per-frame** figure (`ControlSocket.cpp:2041`), read
   off the row and gated with `==` (demand doc §1.2-§1.5). Moving the row to totals would therefore
   multiply the consumer's expectations by the frame count silently. *(An earlier draft of this CR said
   recon §G.2 sketched per-frame rows and contradicted §F.2. It does not: §G.2's example is coherent only
   as totals-on-rows — its `vint` bucket shows `calls: 31` over a 31-frame sample, and its row's `cycles`
   exceeds the same example's `cyclesPerFrame`. Corrected per ruling M5; the resolution below is
   unchanged and was affirmed.)* Rows therefore keep the floor's per-frame meaning;
   `sampleCycles` carries the undivided whole-sample total; and per-row exact totals — the recon's argument
   that they let a client compute a marginal cost from exact integers — are registered as **F-PROF-TOTALS**
   rather than shipped, because `perFrameExact: true` already makes each row's total recoverable by
   multiplication and the false case is reported.
10. **`routines` takes §2.4's nested container spelling.** *(Resolution.)* Recon §G.4 rules the container
    form and recon §G.2's example sketches the flat one. §2.4 decides: the flat spelling belongs to a list
    that **is** the whole result (`checkpoint_list`), and this one is a field of a larger result, exactly
    like `lookup_symbol.otherMatches`. The cost is one line on the consumer's side —
    `prof["routines"]` becomes `prof["routines"]["items"]` — and it is named in the migration list below
    rather than discovered.
11. **`frames` is retargeted, and refused rather than ignored.** *(Resolution.)* Its legacy meaning is the
    divisor window, which is the defect itself (the divisor was the request, not the sample). Here the
    aggregate is always the whole sample, and `frames` bounds the per-frame list; with no ring armed it is
    **refused** with `-32005` (`data.reason = "perFrameNotArmed"`), because a parameter that silently cannot
    affect the answer is worse than one that is rejected — §5's rule against resolving a wrong-state case on
    the client's behalf, applied to a parameter.
12. **Arming is not refused on a running machine.** *(Resolution.)* Recon §F.3(1) offered *"refuses on a
    running machine **or** arms at the next frame boundary"*. Taking the second makes the first unnecessary:
    if the sample's edges are frame boundaries, a free-running arm is exactly as well defined as a paused
    one, and refusing a call the legacy accepted would break a consumer for nothing. Both reads are pure, on
    `emulator/scanlines`'s precedent.

**One place this CR is deliberately weaker than the recon, and it is the honest direction.** Recon §F.3
predicts that with a frame-aligned arm *"`frames: sample - 1` becomes `frames: sample`, and their sample
grows from 30 profiled frames to 31."* This CR does **not** pin that. `run_frames` advances against an
**anchored frame grid**, not from wherever the machine happens to sit: the target is
`frame_boundary_mclk + frames * MCLK_PER_FRAME` (`crates/oracle-core/src/system.rs:918`), and after a
mid-frame stop the anchor and the machine differ by construction (`:920-928`). The frame-boundary hook,
meanwhile, fires at line 224 (`system.rs:1176-1190`), which is not where that grid ticks. So whether a probe
that runs `S` frames sees `S` or `S − 1` **whole** frames depends on the phase between the two, and with a
grid-anchored target against a line-224 boundary the recon's "31" is doubtful rather than merely unpinned. What the amendment guarantees is the part that is structural: every counted
frame is whole, no runt is expressible, and `frameCount` is **reported** so the consumer reads its divisor
instead of computing it. The recon's prediction is recorded here as a prediction to be measured at
acceptance, not as a contract claim.

## 8. The adoption condition

In CR-24/§11.14's two-part structure — suite gates executable in the reference repo, plus a demand-side
acceptance protocol — and for the reason §11.14 states in the catalog's own voice: *"Registration gated on
an unexecutable clause is a condition that gets waived silently."* The entry's closing paragraph is the
normative text; this is the same six clauses with their fixtures named.

**Suite gates, executable here:**

1. **Fragment closure.** A conformant reply passes each of the three fragments **closed**
   (`unevaluatedProperties: false` at test time, §8 item 20), happy path plus one refusal per catalogued
   bound: `top` above `limits.maxProfilerRoutines` → `-32602`; `frames` with no ring armed → `-32005` with
   `data.reason = "perFrameNotArmed"`.
2. **Division exactness**, on a deterministic fixture — a `testrom`-built sample whose frames are identical
   reports `perFrameExact: true` with `sampleCycles == frameCount × totalCycles` **exactly**; and a fixture
   built so the division *cannot* be exact reports `perFrameExact: false`, with no value floored up, no
   substituted denominator and no error. The second half is the one that matters, because it is the clause
   the legacy behaviour would fail.
3. **`calls` exactness**, asserted against a count the **fixture builder** chose rather than against any
   measured number — the discipline the demand side's own derivation used when the hardware disagreed with
   it and they corrected the derivation instead of relaxing the check (demand doc §1.4).
4. **Cause-keying proven by a fixture that takes both interrupts.** HBlank-only puts zero cycles in `vint`;
   VBlank-only puts zero in `hint`; a fixture taking **both** attributes each to its own bucket with neither
   carrying the other's cycles. This is defect 1 turned into a test, and it is the single most important
   gate in the arc.
5. **The disabled path is byte-identical** — the currency gate. Copied from the four-assertion shape already
   in this tree (`frame_boundary_is_state_neutral`, `crates/oracle-core/tests/scanline_capture.rs:269`,
   body through `:301`): equal `export_state_hash`, equal **whole `System` struct**, a
   **liveness control** proving the instrument observed something, and a structural assertion on what it
   observed. Clause three is not optional — without it an instrument that records nothing passes.
6. **Three boots, byte-identical.** Spread exactly `0`, which is the demand side's own noise bar
   (*"every one matched its cost model to the cycle, 3 boots, spread 0."* —
   `aeon/docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-18-scanline-p2-specialization-budget.md:56`, read today) expressed
   as our suite's gate. A spread is never a tolerance: theirs is the old instrument's noise floor, and ours is
   required to be zero.

**Demand-side acceptance protocol** (not a suite gate — the fixture, the ROM and the driver are theirs,
exactly as §11.14's verbatim A1/A2 sweep is): the A/B against their Phase-0 parity corpus, whose **merge SHA
is PENDING on their signal and is not invented here** (demand doc §8 holds the placeholder). Four rules,
from the ruling:

- **`calls` matches exactly.** No stall term, no truncation term, no tolerance column.
- **`cycles` matches exactly on stall-free paths** — the F0-F8 fixture set is the DMA-free control, so a
  delta there has no stall excuse.
- **A stall-touching row reconciles through `stallCycles`, or the arc stops.** It does not widen a bound.
- **The interrupt buckets must legitimately disagree**, by the falsifiable equation *their* `hint` ≈ *our*
  `hint + vint`, with their `vint` structurally `0` against our non-zero one. That is a prediction that can
  fail loudly, which is what makes it evidence that our split is the correct one.

**And the corpus now declares what its own cycles mean.** On receiving the stall finding, the demand side's
in-flight Phase-0 measurement corrected its DMA-stall task **mid-session**: that row now records the old
instrument's blindness as the finding — loud on unmeasurable, citing the recon — instead of shipping a
confident undercount, and every parity-corpus row will carry an *"ideal-cycle by construction"* caveat.
That simplifies the comparison above rather than complicating it: the corpus states its own basis, so a
delta on a stall-touching row starts from a documented difference instead of an argued one.

## Delta 2 (post-adjudication): the accounting semantics the implementation forced

**Delta status: ADJUDICATED 2026-08-19 — ADOPT WITH CHANGES (three MUSTs, three SHOULDs), and the changes
are applied.** The delta ruling is the appended half of `docs/2026-08-19-ruling-cr26.md`. Its substance was
held under a full code audit — including the load-bearing single-source claim for `unattributedCycles` —
and three text defects were caught: **D-M1**, the normative identity summed `interrupts[].cyclesSelf`, a
key the very fragment this delta amends **refused**, so the identity was unexecutable by any conforming
client (fixed by making `cyclesSelf` the bucket's fourth REQUIRED field); **D-M2**, an *only-when*
connective that overclaimed against this section's own floor bound; and **D-M3**, a pre-release predicate
that was false read literally, because the legacy socket serves all three method names and §7 of this
document says so. All three are applied below and in the empyrean text; the two SHOULDs that touch this
document (D-S1, D-S3) are recorded in place.

**Everything above this line is the CR as adjudicated and corrected** (`docs/2026-08-19-ruling-cr26.md`,
ADOPT WITH CHANGES; M1-M6 and S1-S5 applied), except the five precision pointers that name this section:
§1's status header, §1's schema-movement sentence, §5's fragment accounting, §7 item 5, and §12. This
section is a **delta**, not a re-opening: no design decision above changes and no pin moves. It adds three
result fields, one normative identity, one new §6 behaviour and one adoption clause — all of them because
**an implementation was written against the adopted text and its adversarial review found the text
promising something the honest implementation cannot deliver.**

Branch: `oracle-next` `cr26-delta2`, cut from the default branch tip `0b699cb`; the contract half is one
commit on `empyrean` `profiler-amendment`.

### The finding

The adopted §6 said *"Every retired instruction's cycle cost is charged to exactly one routine."* Slice 1's
review (`profiler-s1`, commits `11de661`, `8f701c3`, `8f669fb`, `218ae58` — each carrying its own mutation
ledger) found three places where that promise and an honest accountant part company. The controller ruled
each; the code at `218ae58` is the settled behaviour, and this delta makes the contract say it.

**1. A routine that never returns had no row.** The main loop of every real program returns to nobody, so
under the adopted reading it got no row at all — while its cycles still counted toward `sampleCycles`. The
rows did not add up, and the one routine a profiler most obviously ought to show was the one it hid.

**2. Where the fold happens is load-bearing, and the ruling's own wording had to be refined to serve it.**
The ruling said still-open frames fold at report time. Implemented that way it would sweep in the partial
tail **after the last boundary** — precisely the span `sampleCycles` excludes by construction — so a
report-time fold breaks the identity it exists to establish. The settled behaviour is a **boundary
checkpoint**: each frame boundary folds every live frame's accrual into its row and zeroes the accrual.
Same observable outcome for the consumer, exact totals for the arithmetic. That deviation is the
implementation's, it was flagged for review rather than taken quietly, and the controller ruled it in.

**3. Some cycles genuinely belong to no bucket, and dropping them would be a silent shortfall.** An
interrupt already in flight when the sample opened may not have a bucket opened for it — the adopted §6
says so in a MUST NOT, and that MUST NOT is *right*: a bucket whose entry was never observed reports a cost
the sample cannot account for. But its in-sample cycles are real and are inside `sampleCycles`. They now
have a name: **`unattributedCycles`**. It is not a fudge factor; it is the term that lets the identity be
stated as an equality instead of an inequality, and it has exactly one source, named in the text so a
second source cannot be quietly added to it.

Two consequences the demand side must read before it reads a row:

- **`calls` counts COMPLETED invocations.** A routine live at the close has a row carrying every cycle it
  accrued, with `calls: 0`. That is the main-loop row's *expected* shape, not a defect, and the alternative
  — inventing a completion nobody observed — is the fabrication class this whole CR exists to refuse.
- **The empty sample stays coherent, and now says so.** At `frameCount: 0` every divided figure is `0`,
  `perFrameExact` is `true`, and `unattributedCycles` is necessarily `0` as well — nothing is committed
  before the sample's second boundary — so the identity's degenerate case reads `0 + 0 + 0 == 0`. The two
  counters are the exception and may be non-zero there, because they count since arming. Added per ruling
  **D-S2**, whose point is that the degenerate case is the first thing a reader sanity-checks.
- **`perFrameExact` will read `false` in nearly every real sample** (review note N10). Almost nothing on a
  running game divides evenly across a multi-frame window. The text is working as written; saying so here
  is cheaper than letting a consumer read the common case as an alarm. A gate that needs `==` should drive
  a deterministic fixture or compare undivided figures.

And one acceptance-protocol note (review note N7): **a single 68k→VDP DMA can hold the bus across more than
one boundary**, so a per-frame figure over such a sample is *diluted* — every frame it spans takes a share
of one indivisible act. A consumer comparing against a per-frame ideal reads `sampleCycles` with
`frameCount` instead. That is a property of dividing by frames at all, not of the accounting, and it is now
in §6 beside the divisor rather than in a handoff nobody re-reads.

### What the delta changes, exactly

**(a) §6 — the attribution promise, refined, with the identity normative.** *"Charged to exactly one
routine"* becomes: every cycle the sample retired is attributed **exactly once** — to a routine row, to an
interrupt bucket, or to `unattributedCycles` — and

> Σ `routines[].cyclesSelf` + Σ `interrupts[].cyclesSelf` + `unattributedCycles` == `sampleCycles`

over the **undivided** sample, exactly, with no tolerance. Stated on *self* because inclusive time
double-counts by construction, and because a parent's share of a callee still in flight across a boundary
reaches the parent's row only when the callee returns — self has no such lag. The §6 text also gives the
**wire-side** form, since rows and buckets travel divided: `(Σ routines[].cyclesSelf + Σ
interrupts[].cyclesSelf) × frameCount + unattributedCycles == sampleCycles`. It is **guaranteed to close
exactly when `perFrameExact` is `true`**; when it is `false` each divided figure is floored, so the
reconstruction may fall **short — by at most `frameCount − 1` per summed figure, never over** — and it may
still close on the nose anyway, because that flag reports truncation in *every* divided figure in the reply
and not only in the two this sum touches. That bound is stated rather than left for a client to rediscover,
and it is the practical consequence of N10 above. *(**Superseded in part by Delta 3**, which publishes
`cyclesSelfTotal` on rows and buckets and so gives the identity a wire form with no `perFrameExact`
condition and no bound at all. Everything in this paragraph stays true **of the divided view**, which is
the only thing it was ever true of.)*

*(Both halves of that paragraph are post-ruling corrections.* **D-M1**: the identity's bucket term was
`interrupts[].cyclesSelf`, and the `interruptBucket` fragment was `{cycles, stallCycles, calls}` with
`additionalProperties: false` — so the contract directed a client to compute a sum and then refused every
reply that would let it. The in-tree gate closed only because it reads the internal `Report`, whose buckets
*do* carry `self_cycles`, which is also why the fix is cheap: **`cyclesSelf` is now the bucket's fourth
REQUIRED field**, covered by the same pre-release argument as the other three. Verified in the
implementation before adopting it rather than after: `report()` divides the bucket's `self_cycles` through
the **same `div` closure**, by the same `n`, feeding the same `exact` flag as the row's — so it lands on
round-1 M2's divisor by construction and cannot drift from it. The declined alternative was to restate the
identity over keys the wire already had, which would have forfeited client-side reconciliation — the very
thing this delta's typed counters are justified by. **D-M2**: *"closes exactly only when `perFrameExact` is
`true`"* let a conformance author derive `!perFrameExact ⟹ the sum misses`, which this paragraph's own
floor bound refutes, since the flag also trips on `calls` and `stallCycles` truncating.*)

**(b) §6 — an eleventh behaviour: the two robustness counters, typed.** `abandonedFrames` (frames torn off
the shadow stack without completing — cycles kept, calls not) and `depthExceeded` (calls declined at the
stack's bound — cycles charged to the innermost tracked frame). Both are **counted since arming**, not
since the sample opened — a pin added per ruling **D-S2** and verified in the implementation, whose
opening-boundary arm zeroes frame accruals and suppresses buckets while touching neither counter. They are
facts about the instrument's whole watch, so an event in the pre-open span still shows, which is the honest
direction: the alternative hides a lost stack behind a boundary. Both are **facts about the measurement, not
about the program**, both REQUIRED even when `0` (§2.3's reason: absence and zero must not both mean
"nothing happened"), and both **typed fields rather than caveat prose**, because a client reconciling rows
against the identity wants them machine-readable. The `caveat` names whichever is non-zero and MUST NOT be
emitted for them when both are `0` — §2.4's advisory applied, so the field stays a signal.

**(c) Four fragment fields, and the fragment count does not move.** *(Eight after Delta 3, in twelve
places; the count is enumerated in §11.16 rather than tallied in a heading.)* `unattributedCycles`,
`abandonedFrames` and `depthExceeded` are added to `get_profiler_frames`' **existing** fragment as REQUIRED
integers with `minimum: 0`, and — per **D-M1** — `cyclesSelf` joins them inside `interruptBucket` on the
same terms. Parsed before and after: the `methods` object holds **36 fragments (37 keys with
`$comment`) on both sides**, and no other fragment is touched. The fragment's `$comment` gains the identity,
the completed-calls rule and the caveat convention as its prose-only clauses.

**(d) Why three REQUIRED additions are safe here, and would not be later.** A required result key is the one
direction D5's additivity does not cover: a client validating against a vendored schema survives a new key,
but a **server** that omits it stops conforming. It is safe now for a reason that expires — **no server
speaking this bus implements these three methods, and no client has ever received a reply any fragment
governed.** *(Scoped per **D-M3**, and the scoping is the whole argument: read without it the sentence is
simply false, and §7 item 4 of this document is the proof — the legacy socket serves all three method names
today and the demand side's probe takes its replies every run. Those are the pre-§11.16 floor: never
stamped, never conformant, governed by no fragment because none existed.)* The fragments were written ahead
of the handler, which is §8 item 20's ordering working as intended, so the only **conforming**
implementation a required addition can break is one that does not exist. **An old-shape reply refusing against the amended
fragment is the wanted behaviour**, and the harness asserts it three times. The day after the first server
ships one of these replies, the same edit is a breaking change; the delta is taken now for that reason.

**(e) The adoption condition gains clause (7): the identity as an executable gate.** It is asserted in the
reference repo by `the_rows_account_for_the_sample_exactly`
(`crates/oracle-core/tests/profiler.rs`, `profiler-s1` at `218ae58`) over **eight** fixture shapes chosen so
each stresses a different attribution path: a k-times leaf, a two-level tree, recursion, a jump-dispatch, an
idle-in-routine, both interrupts, a mode switch, and a DMA stall. A leak anywhere — a frame whose cycles
never reach a row, a checkpoint that double-counts, a suppressed bucket that is not accounted for — shows up
as a mismatch in one line. Clauses 1-7 are now the executable half of the condition.

### Two things this delta deliberately does **not** change

- **The handler-row sentence stays as adopted, and it is now conformed to rather than amended.** §6 already
  says *"a handler's entry address still gets its own `routines[]` row"*. Slice 1 shipped without one; the
  review filed it as a blocker (B1) precisely **because** the contract already said otherwise, and the fix
  made the code conform. Checked for this delta: no text change is needed, and none is made. The suite even
  had a test pinning the violation as expected behaviour — the contract was the thing that was right.
- **The retroactive-bucket MUST NOT stays as adopted, for the same reason.** M3's suppression semantics —
  an interrupt in flight at the opening boundary gets no bucket — are exactly what the adopted sentence
  requires, and the delta's `unattributedCycles` exists to make that suppression *accountable* rather than
  to soften it. Verified, unchanged, and said here so a reader does not go looking for an edit that isn't
  there.

### Two items handed to later slices rather than settled here

**A wire-mapping question for the bus slice.** The core's report carries two stall totals the wire shape
does not name: **`sample_stall_cycles`, which is undivided**, and **`total_stall_cycles`, which is that
figure divided by `frameCount`** — the pairing `sampleCycles`/`totalCycles` already uses. *(An earlier draft
of this section called both undivided; corrected per ruling **D-S3**, verified in `report()`, where
`total_stall_cycles = div(self.committed_stall)` runs through the same divisor as every other divided
figure.)* Whether either reaches the wire is a mapping question for the handler slice, not a contract
defect. Nothing is contradicted by leaving it open: the adopted §6 and the fragment promise `stallCycles` on
rows, on buckets and on `perFrame` rows only, so no machine-level stall total was ever promised at result
level.

**A code cleanup for the same slice, recorded so the evidence travels — ruling D-S1.** This delta's
load-bearing factual claim is that `unattributedCycles` has **exactly one source**. The audit held it, and
also found a **second write site that is dead**: `charge()`'s refused-root fallback
(`crates/oracle-core/src/profiler.rs`, the `else` after the empty-stack root push) adds to
`pending_unattributed` and its comment describes itself as the stack-capped case — but in the adjudicator's
words it is *"unreachable today: an empty stack always accepts the root push (`MAX_DEPTH` = 16384 ≥ 1)"*,
and the other candidates were each verified to feed rows rather than the escape hatch (*"abandoned frames
checkpoint into their rows before the uncompleted pop; depth-cap cycles charge to the innermost tracked
frame; the opening boundary discards pre-open pendings entirely"*). So the contract's enumeration is
observationally true today. The hazard is a refactor: *"a refactor that makes that arm reachable would
silently mint a second source with no test to catch it and the contract's enumeration already stale."*
**This is code, not contract, and no code is edited from this docs round** — it is registered for the
bus-surface slice, which should make the arm's unreachability explicit or assert it, so that "exactly one
source" keeps being a claim something enforces.

## Delta 3: the undivided set, and an identity that needs no caveat

**Raised and applied 2026-08-19, on branch `cr26-delta3` (cut from the default tip `59af622`), with the
contract half as one commit on `empyrean` `profiler-amendment`.** This is **the last delta the pre-release
window allows**, so it carries the whole of the change at once rather than the one field that was asked
for. Everything above stands; this section refines the division story that Delta 2's §(a) established, and
where it does, it says which sentence it supersedes.

### What was asked, and what the controller took

The demand side's fourth statement (`docs/2026-08-19-aeon-streaming-demand.md` §C2, transcribing their
packet at `CHOKE-DIAGNOSIS.md:607-613`) asked for **one** field:

> *"**C2. Exact total call counts, not integer-rounded per-frame averages.** `calls` is a per-frame average
> truncated to an integer. For a routine invoked 4.53 times per logic tick it reports `2`;
> `TileCache_DecompressBlock` and `S4LZ_DecompressDict` are called 1:1 and report `2` and `1`. Every rate
> in this packet therefore had to come from an engine-side monotone counter that happened to exist
> (`Block_Stage_Gen`) or from cache geometry. **Ask:** a `callsTotal` integer alongside the per-frame
> average, over the sampled window."*

**And it is our gap, not an inherited one** — the recon says so before it says anything else
(`docs/2026-08-19-streaming-asks-recon.md` §3.1): our `report()` divides counts with plain integer
division, so the same routine reports the same unusable `2`, arrived at honestly. We beat the reference in
exactly one respect that does not help them: we never floor **up**, so a routine invoked once in the sample
reports `0` where theirs fabricates `1`.

The recon registered the wider question rather than folding it in — **F-PROFILER-EXACT-TOTALS**, §3.6 — and
**the controller took it**. The reasoning it turns on is the asymmetry the recon named: a `==` gate on a
**count** fails **loudly** (4.53 becomes 2, which is why this ask exists at all), while a `==` gate on
**cycles** fails **quietly** — a shortfall of at most `frameCount − 1` against a row of ~51,357 cycles reads
exactly like agreement. Shipping the loud fix and leaving the quiet one, inside a window that shuts once,
would have been the worse of the two mistakes.

### The shape

Each **routine row** and each **interrupt bucket** carries four more REQUIRED integers —
**`cyclesTotal`**, **`cyclesSelfTotal`**, **`stallCyclesTotal`**, **`callsTotal`** — the same four
quantities over the whole sample, **undivided**, keyed identically to their per-frame partners and standing
beside them rather than replacing them. The divided figures stay REQUIRED and unchanged: division-inside is
a pinned property of this surface and the migrating consumer's arithmetic rests on it (§10's migration
list is unaffected).

Each pair is tied by an invariant that makes a total more than a second number: when `frameCount > 0`,
`divided == total / frameCount` under integer division, so

> `divided × frameCount ≤ total < (divided + 1) × frameCount`

— **a total bounds its partner's truncation.** With `frameCount: 0` there are no rows, so the relation is
never left undefined on a reply that has any.

**Not on `perFrame[]` rows.** They are whole-frame totals with no per-routine breakdown and are already
undivided; a total there would be a second spelling of the same number. That is a bound on where the fields
may appear, and the harness enforces it as a negative control rather than trusting the prose.

### The prize, and it is the headline

**The reconciliation identity now has a wire form that is unconditionally exact:**

> Σ `routines[].cyclesSelfTotal` + Σ `interrupts[].cyclesSelfTotal` + `unattributedCycles` ==
> `sampleCycles`

— **no `perFrameExact` condition, no floor bound, every term a REQUIRED key of the reply.** Delta 2 could
only offer a divided reconstruction hedged three ways, and delta 2's own ruling (**D-M2**) made the hedging
sharper rather than smaller. **That hedging is now a property of the divided *view* alone**, which is where
it always belonged: §6 states both forms and says plainly which one a client should check. A suite can gate
the identity on any sample, not only on a fixture engineered to divide evenly — and the harness below
**computes it from a hand-built reply whose `perFrameExact` is `false`**, which is the demonstration that
the condition is really gone rather than merely reworded.
