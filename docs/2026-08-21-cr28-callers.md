## 1. Why this is being raised

### 1.1 The instrument answers *how much*, and the client needs *who*

§11.16 (CR-26) gave this bus an exact per-invocation CPU-cycle accountant: rows keyed by routine entry
address, inclusive and self cycles, a stall subset, counts, undivided partners for all four, two interrupt
buckets keyed by cause, and a reconciliation identity a client can check exactly. What a row cannot express
is a **caller**. `routines[]` is keyed by entry address — `protocol.md` §6, *"rows are keyed by entry
address, never by symbol"* — so a routine reached from four places is one row with one number, and the
consumer's actual question (*which* of the four is driving this burst) has no expressible answer.

The requesting client is **aeon**, and their use is on record in their own tree: burst attribution for the
streaming-variance work — *"who invokes `S4LZ_DecompressDict` / `TileCache_FindStagedBlock`, per 31-frame
window, with callee self-time attributed by caller"* (`CR28-DEMAND.md` at `6edb08ef`). This is the third of
the three streaming asks the 2026-08-19 recon answered. The first (attribution under preemption) was already
correct by construction and needed a witness fixture, not a contract change. The second (`callsTotal` as an
exact integer) landed as a delta inside §11.16. Only this one is CR-sized, and §4.3 of the recon says why in
three parts: a new `set_profiler` param changes **what the instrument does**; a new `initialize.limits` key
changes what a server **advertises about itself**; and a nested shape with its own ordering and truncation
rules is new **surface**, not a new column.

### 1.2 The cheap answer is barred, and the reason is a migration this catalog already sold

Re-keying rows by `(addr, caller)` is the cheapest accumulator change of the three shapes the recon priced,
and it is the one it rejects outright. §11.16's migration story rests on a consumer's address-keyed lookup
continuing to work; a consumer doing `rows[addr]` against a re-keyed reply would suddenly get **N rows per
address, silently, with every one of them smaller than the figure it used to read**. That is the exact
silent-wrong-number class this family was re-specified to end. The demand asks for *"`callers: [...]` per
routine row"*, not for a re-key, and this CR takes the ask literally.

### 1.3 The mechanical claim the design rests on, restated as something refutable

The recon's §4.1 verifies aeon's *"your shadow-stack design may get this nearly for free"* hint and states
it precisely: the caller is available **at the push**, as one `self.stack.last()` read, and the per-caller
cost is proportional to **calls, not to instructions** — *because* cycles are folded at invocation
boundaries rather than keyed per step. A sampling profiler, or any design keying cycles by PC on every step,
would need the caller at every step, which is the expensive version of this feature. The hot path
(`charge()`, every retired step) is untouched; the added work is one word stored per push and one extra map
entry-and-add on the checkpoint/pop path.

**This CR does not re-verify that trace and does not need to.** It is an implementation-cost claim about the
reference server, it is anchored in the recon at `profiler-s1` `b46bbbc`, and nothing in the contract text
below depends on it being true — the wire shape would be the same if the accounting were expensive. It is
reproduced here because it is the reason the lens is armed for the **reply's** sake rather than the
accumulator's, which is a distinction §7.3 turns into contract text.

### 1.4 The demand's one change to the design, and why it is the interesting part

The recon's §4.1 named three edges where a caller has no address, and proposed the cheapest honest spelling:
*"omit `callerAddr` on that entry and let its absence mean 'entered from an interrupt'. The latter fits
§2.3's presence discipline better and needs no new enum on the wire."*

The consumer read that and refused it, in their own words:

> **Absence collapsing three meanings is the one thing I'd change.** Interrupt-entered vs root-edge vs
> depth-cap are different facts, and for streaming attribution the interrupt-vs-mainline distinction is one
> I will actually consume (a Tile_Cache_Fill edge from VBlank means something different from one from the
> game loop).

Their proposal — a required `entryKind: "interrupt" | "root" | "depthCap"` whenever `callerAddr` is absent —
is what this CR specifies, and their **stated fallback floor** if the enum spelling loses adjudication is
*"interrupt-entered edges keyed distinctly from the other two absence cases, however spelled."* That floor is
carried into §8.2 verbatim so the adjudicator can rule against the spelling without having to invent a
replacement.

Two things also arrived binding and are recorded here so a later reader does not mistake them for open:

- **Per-edge `callsTotal` is kept**, for row symmetry. *"Symmetry with the routine row matters — an
  asymmetric tuple invites exactly the mis-read your docs warn about elsewhere."*
- **Per-edge `stallCycles` is EXPLICITLY DECLINED** — *"measured flat and VBlank-owned at every state I've
  taken, it would be dead weight."* A drafter adding one would be contradicting the demand rather than
  gold-plating it. This CR therefore bars the key mechanically (`additionalProperties: false` on the edge
  shape) rather than leaving it merely undeclared, so the decision is enforced instead of remembered; §6.3
  case 29 is the message that proves it.

work lands once per call and once per checkpoint — and is armed because **the reply** grows by rows ×
edges. Both reach the same answer from opposite directions, and stating which one applies keeps a later
reader from optimising the wrong half.

observed edge and the cap decides how many are *sent* — which is exactly what makes a row's `callersTotal`
the true count of distinct callers rather than the count that survived a ceiling. A client reading
`callersTotal: 40` beside a nine-entry list is being told the truth about its own instrument.

and `perFrame` keep §2.4's nested container spelling. The fragment count. The rows-keyed-by-entry-address

either, on §11.16's own bound — whole-frame totals carry no per-routine breakdown, so there is nothing for
a caller to attach to. And §8's checklist gains **no** item: item 22 is bus-wide policy, this is one
family's second lens, and a checklist that grows with every feature stops being a checklist.

## 7. Pins and decisions

Eight places where the recon left two readings, or where this CR reaches a **different** answer than the
recon recommended. Each names the alternative it did not take. Four are departures from the recon; **none**
departs from the demand-side answer, which is binding.

### 7.1 The list spelling — *(DEPARTS from the recon, and the departure exists because the recon's recommendation collides with a MUST it did not cite. Presented as an open question in §8.1 rather than settled here.)*

The recon recommends *"a plain array plus a per-row `callersTruncated` boolean"*, objecting — correctly —
that a full §2.4 container inside an item of a container is a third level of `{items, total, returned,
truncated}` scaffolding for a top-N list. What it does not cite is that **§2.4 clause (a) is a MUST**: *"A
list bounded by policy MUST carry `total` and `truncated` … `returned` is required with them."* `callers` is
policy-bounded by construction — `maxProfilerCallers` is a number the server picked. So the recon's
recommendation is not merely "less scaffolding"; taken as written it is a **carve-out from a normative MUST**,
and adopting it silently would be exactly the kind of quiet weakening this catalog's §11.9 exists to record.

This CR takes a **third** answer the recon did not consider, and §2.4 already blesses it: the **flat
spelling**. §2.4's *"Two spellings, and which one applies"* gives a container object for a list that is a
*field*, and a flat form — *"the array under its own name beside `total`, `returned`, `limit`, `truncated`
and `cursor?` as siblings"* — for a list that **is** the whole result, with the note that *"the two spellings
carry the identical field set and the identical rules; only the nesting differs."* Scoping the flat spelling
to a **row** rather than to a whole result satisfies clause (a)'s field set exactly, costs no third level of
`{items: …}`, and is a smaller extension of an existing spelling than a carve-out from a MUST would be.

The one thing that is genuinely new is the *scope* the flat spelling is applied at, and §8.1 puts that to
the adjudicator with all three candidate spellings written out, because — in the recon's own words — *"what
must not happen is the question going unasked."*

### 7.2 The `callers` echo is capability-conditional, not REQUIRED — *(a constraint the recon did not reach, resolved here)*

`set_profiler.result` requires `["enabled","perFrame"]` and `get_profiler.result` requires four keys. Adding
`callers` to either `required` list is the natural symmetry and is **barred**: `"profiler": true` is
advertised by a shipped server today, so §11.16's pre-release licence — *"an old-shape reply refusing against
the amended fragment is the wanted behaviour today and would be a breaking change the day after the first
server ships one"* — has expired. A required addition would make every server that does not implement this
lens non-conformant, which is precisely the direction D5's additivity does **not** cover.

The resolution is the catalog's own existing shape for exactly this: `limits.maxWriteLen` and
`maxHashLen` are **schema-optional with a conditional requirement stated in prose** (*"REQUIRED when the
server advertises `emulator/write_memory`"*), because `limits.required` cannot express *required iff the
server implements X*. `maxProfilerRoutines` and `maxProfilerFrames` already ride that same pattern. CR-28
applies it one notch further out: the requirement is conditioned on a **lens** rather than on a method, and
**`limits.maxProfilerCallers`'s presence is the capability signal** — a client branches on it, per D5's *"no
client ever branches on a version integer at runtime — it branches on capabilities."*

The consequence is stated in the fragments rather than left to be discovered: **absence of the `callers`
echo means *this server has no caller lens*, and `false` means *the lens exists and is off*.** Those are
different facts and the wire distinguishes them — §2.3's presence discipline honoured rather than waived.
*The alternative not taken* is a `capabilities.profilerCallers` boolean beside `capabilities.profiler`; it is
a second way to say the same thing, and the limits key has to exist anyway for the bound.

### 7.3 Opt-in for the **reply's** sake — *(sharpens the recon, which gave both reasons without ranking them)*

The recon offers *"not because the accumulator cost is scary (it is not), but because the reply grows by rows
× N sub-objects."* This CR makes the ranking normative text because it **inverts `perFrame`'s** reason —
`perFrame` is armed for memory across a long sample; `callers` is armed for reply size, its accumulator being
proportional to calls rather than instructions. Stating which one applies is what stops a later reader
optimising the wrong half; §11.18's second ★ carries it.

### 7.4 `topCallers` exists, and it is what gives `callersNotArmed` a subject — *(a gap in the recon, filled)*

The recon writes the refusal as *"`get_profiler_frames{...}` refusing `-32005` `callersNotArmed` mirrors
`perFrameNotArmed`"* — with the params **elided**. The elision hides a real question, because
`perFrameNotArmed` is not a refusal of a *read*: it is the refusal of the **`frames` param**, on the stated
reason that *"a parameter that cannot affect the answer is worse than one that is rejected."* A design with
no read-side param has nothing to refuse, and `callersNotArmed` would be a dead code.

So `get_profiler_frames` gains **`topCallers`**, and the mirror is exact in every part: it *bounds* the list
(as `frames` bounds the per-frame list and `top` bounds `routines.items`); it is refused above the advertised
cap with `-32602`, **never clamped**; and sent against an unarmed sample it is `-32005 callersNotArmed`.

**It does not gate presence.** `callers[]` appears **iff armed**, exactly as `perFrame` appears iff armed —
`frames` narrows an armed ring and never conjures one. *The alternative not taken* is a boolean read-side
`callers?` request flag, which would make emission depend on two independently settable gates, put a second
weaker refusal case beside this one, and depart from the `perFrame` precedent the demand accepted this
design *for*.

### 7.5 The refusal is `get_profiler_frames`' alone, and `get_profiler` gains only an echo — *(derived, and it corrects a plausible reading)*

It is natural to assume the caller lens attaches to `get_profiler`, since that is the read whose name says
*profiler*. It does not: `get_profiler` returns `{enabled, perFrame, framesRecorded, routineCount}` and
**carries no routine rows at all** — the rows live on `get_profiler_frames`. So `callers[]` can only appear
on `get_profiler_frames`, `callersNotArmed` is **structurally unreachable** on `get_profiler`, and what
`get_profiler` gains is one echo: `callers`, the third arming fact, beside `enabled` and `perFrame`. That is
in the fragment's own `$comment` so the next reader does not have to re-derive it.

### 7.6 No per-row `limit` sibling — *(a decision §2.4 leaves open, taken)*

§2.4 makes `limit` **OPTIONAL** ("reports the ceiling the server actually applied"). It is not emitted here:
the applied ceiling is **one number for the entire reply** — the client's `topCallers`, or the advertised cap
— so repeating it on every row would be a constant field, which is §2.4's own constant-caveat advisory
(*"documentation wearing signal's clothes"*) in field form. A client that sent `topCallers` already knows it;
one that did not reads `limits.maxProfilerCallers`.

### 7.7 `maxProfilerCallers` bounds the **reply**, not retention — *(a distinction neither source drew)*

`maxProfilerFrames` is a **ring depth**: rows past it are gone, and no `total` can be honest about them.
`maxProfilerCallers` is `maxProfilerRoutines`' kind: the accumulator keeps every observed edge and the cap
decides how many are *sent*. That is what makes `callersTotal` a true count of distinct callers rather than
the count that survived a ceiling — and it is worth pinning, because the two caps sit adjacent in `limits`
and an implementer copying the wrong neighbour produces a `callersTotal` that is quietly a lie.

### 7.8 `callersNotArmed` is registered where `perFrameNotArmed` is, and §5 is not edited — *(derived)*

§5's `-32005` paragraph carries an **open** `data.reason` string with an *e.g.* list
(`machineRunning`, `checkpointCapReached`, `unknownCheckpoint`, `watchCapReached`), and the schema types
`reason` as a plain string with the same three named as examples. **`perFrameNotArmed` is in neither** — it
lives in §6's profiler blockquote and in its own param's fragment description, which is the register this
catalog actually uses for method-local discriminants. `callersNotArmed` is registered the same way, in the
same two places. *The alternative not taken* is extending §5's example list; adding `callersNotArmed` there
while its own sibling `perFrameNotArmed` is absent would make the list look like an enumeration it is not,
and closing `reason` into an enum is a separate bus-wide change nobody asked for.

**Carried from the demand side without reopening** (their answers are binding; §1.4 records them): in-row
`callers[]` over the separate method; the tuple exactly as proposed, `callsTotal` included; **no per-edge
stall**; and `entryKind` required whenever `callerAddr` is absent.

**One attribution stated precisely, because this register's failure mode is true-sounding claims.** The
sentence *"one absence must not mean three facts"* is **this CR's** compression of the consumer's position,
not a sentence their anchor contains. What their anchor contains is: *"Absence must carry its reason: when
`callerAddr` is absent, a required `entryKind` … on the edge — one absence must not mean three facts,"* and
the floor position quoted in §8.2. The contract text in §3(d) is written against that, generalised off their
engine's names.

## 8. Open questions for adjudication

Three, and the first two are the ones the brief for this CR named as mandatory to ask.

### 8.1 The bounded-list spelling, one level deeper than this catalog has gone — **three candidates, all written out**

`callers` is a **policy-bounded list that is a field of an item of a container**. §2.4 has never been applied
at that depth. Three spellings are available and the CR is drafted at **(γ)**; swapping to either of the
others is mechanical and the CR's harness cases 16–19 move with it.

**(α) The recon's recommendation — a plain array plus one boolean.**

```json
"callers": { "type": "array", "items": { … } },
"callersTruncated": { "type": "boolean" }
```

*For:* smallest surface; a top-N list of ≤ `maxProfilerCallers` entries is walkable, so `returned` buys
little and `total` is the only genuinely new fact. *Against:* **§2.4 clause (a) is a MUST** and this drops
two of its three required companions. Choosing it is a deliberate carve-out and should be recorded as one,
not slipped in as a simplification.

**(β) The full §2.4 nested container, one level down.**

```json
"callers": {
  "allOf": [{ "$ref": "#/$defs/boundedList" }],
  "properties": { "items": { "type": "array", "items": { … } } }
}
```

*For:* zero new precedent — it is the spelling `routines` and `perFrame` already use, reached by `$ref`, so
a client's container-handling code works unchanged at both depths. *Against:* `{items, total, returned,
truncated}` repeated **per row**; a client reaching an edge writes
`routines.items[i].callers.items[j]` — three levels of `.items` — and the recon's objection lands.

**(γ) §2.4's FLAT spelling, scoped to a row — what the CR is drafted at.**

```json
"callers": { "type": "array", "items": { … } },
"callersTotal": { "type": "integer", "minimum": 0 },
"callersReturned": { "type": "integer", "minimum": 0 },
"callersTruncated": { "type": "boolean" }
```

tied by `dependentRequired` so all four arrive or none does. *For:* §2.4 clause (a)'s **field set satisfied
in full**, no MUST carved out, and no third `.items` — it is the spelling §2.4 already defines, applied at a
scope §2.4 has not yet named. *Against:* §2.4 attaches the flat form to *"a list that **is** the whole
result"*, so applying it to a row is a small widening of when flat applies, and it spends four row keys
where (α) spends two.

**What the adjudicator is being asked:** whether clause (a)'s field set binds a list at this depth (→ β or
γ), and if so whether the flat spelling may be scoped to a row (→ γ) or must nest (→ β); or whether a
top-N list of this size earns an explicit carve-out from clause (a) (→ α), **recorded as a carve-out**.
The CR has no stake beyond wanting the question ruled on deliberately.

### 8.2 The `entryKind` enum spelling — with the demand side's floor, stated by them

The CR specifies the consumer's own three-value enum, `"interrupt" | "root" | "depthCap"`, and carries it as
the demand-side position because that is what the shape answer instructs. Their **floor** if the spelling
loses, in their own words:

> If the adjudicator rules against the enum, the fallback I need is: interrupt-entered edges keyed
> distinctly from the other two absence cases, however spelled.

Two alternatives satisfy that floor, and one of them is arguably better than what is drafted:

- **A five-value split by cause: `"hint" | "vint" | "root" | "depthCap"`.** §6's own strongest profiler rule
  is that *"`interrupts` is keyed by interrupt CAUSE, never by handler address,"* and *"a silently wrong
  number is worse than a missing one"* — yet `entryKind: "interrupt"` **collapses `hint` and `vint`**, the
  exact two things that section refuses to collapse anywhere else. Splitting them is strictly more
  informative, satisfies the consumer's floor twice over, and lets an interrupt-entered edge join directly
  to the bucket it came from. It costs a client that only wants interrupt-versus-mainline one extra value to
  test.
- **`entryKind: "interrupt"` plus a sibling `entryCause: "hint" | "vint"`.** Keeps the consumer's literal
  spelling and adds the cause beside it; costs one more key on the rarest rows.

**A related sub-question the CR did not take a position on:** whether `entryKind` should be *permitted* when
`callerAddr` **is** present, with a fourth value marking the inferred-root edge (§7's caveat: a real
mid-routine address that is not an entry point). The CR bars it — `entryKind` is present **iff** `callerAddr`
is absent, enforced by `if`/`then`/`else` — because nobody asked for it and the caveat is a documented
property rather than an unrepresentable one. It is registered instead as **`F-PROFILER-ROOT-EDGE-KIND`**. If
the adjudicator wants that fact typed, this is the round to say so, since widening the biconditional later is
a shape change and not merely a new value.

### 8.3 The tuple's missing undivided partners — **flagged, not reopened**

The banked tuple carries `calls` **and** `callsTotal`, but `cycles`/`cyclesSelf` with **no** undivided
partners. That is asymmetric against the routine row, which carries all four pairs — and the demand's own
stated reason for keeping `callsTotal` was *"symmetry with the routine row matters."*

The consequence is precise and worth having on the record: of the two normative edge-versus-row sums this CR
specifies, the `callsTotal` one is checkable **exactly** and the `cyclesSelf` one only **up to floor error** —
and `cyclesSelf` is the field the consumer named as *"the money field for my burst work."* §11.16's own
argument for publishing the undivided set applies verbatim one level down, and with more force: *"A `==` gate
on a count fails loudly … a `==` gate on cycles fails quietly,"* and an edge is a **smaller denominator** than
a row, so its divided figures are floored more often, not less.

**This is flagged and not reopened.** The consumer declined per-edge **stall** explicitly and on measured
grounds; they were **not** asked about undivided cycle partners and declined nothing there. Adding
`cyclesTotal`/`cyclesSelfTotal` to the edge tuple would be additive under D5 and is registered as
**`F-PROFILER-EDGE-UNDIVIDED`**. The adjudicator may fold it in now (two more optional-by-arming integers,
no shape change, and it makes clause 4 of the adoption condition exactly checkable rather than
bound-checkable) or leave it to the first client or gate that cannot be written without it. The CR's own
preference, stated so it can be overruled: **fold it in**, because the alternative ships a normative sum that
no suite gate can assert with `==`.
